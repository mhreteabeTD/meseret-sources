**Learning outcomes**
Up-on successful completion of this unit, you will be able to:
- predict topics of listening texts;
- identify specific and main ideas from listening texts;
- talk about dry season farming activities in their areas;
- identify specific and major ideas of a reading passage;
- answer reference and inference questions on a reading passage;
- analyse the message of a poem about the winter season;
- use appropriate season- and weather-related expressions;
- use the Simple Present Tense to describe habitual actions and what happens in the dry season;
- employ comparative and superlative forms of adjectives to describe the weather and seasons;
- pronounce weather related adjectives correctly;
- talk about the present weather; and
- write short descriptive paragraphs about weather and seasons.

### Week 5: Day 1 — Lesson 1, Part 1

[listening text in teacher's guide] (a text about *Dry Season*; not printed in the Student Book)

**Activity 1:** You are going to listen to a text about *Dry Season*. Before you listen to the text, discuss the following questions with a friend.

1. What do you know about seasons?

**Answer:** (model answer) Seasons are the parts of the year that have their own type of weather, such as hot, cold, dry or rainy. Some countries have four seasons (spring, summer, autumn, winter); tropical countries like Ethiopia mainly have a dry season and a rainy season.
**Why:** Open pre-listening discussion question; any sensible description of seasons as periods of the year with typical weather is acceptable.

2. What is the difference between dry season and rainy season?

**Answer:** (model answer) In the dry season there is little or no rain, the air is dry, the sky is usually clear and sunny, and rivers and grass dry up. In the rainy season there is a lot of rain, the sky is cloudy, and rivers are full and plants grow green.
**Why:** Open discussion question; the answer contrasts low rainfall/low humidity (dry) with heavy rainfall (rainy), which the listening text and Lesson 6 example ("The dry season is a yearly period of low rainfall in the tropics") also describe.

**Activity 2:** These words and phrases are used in the listening text. In pairs, practise their pronunciation and guess their meanings.

| | |
|---|---|
| • climate | • bushfires |
| • temperature | • hemisphere |
| • precipitation | • tropics |
| • weather | • tropical rain belt |

**Answer:** (model answer)
- climate – the usual weather of a place over a long time
- temperature – how hot or cold something is
- precipitation – water that falls from the sky (rain, snow, hail)
- weather – the condition of the air (sun, rain, wind, heat) at a particular time
- bushfires – fires that burn out of control in bush, grass or forest
- hemisphere – one half of the Earth (northern or southern, divided by the equator)
- tropics – the hot area of the Earth near the equator
- tropical rain belt – the band of cloud and heavy rain near the equator that moves north and south during the year
**Why:** Guessing/pronunciation activity with no fixed key; these are the standard dictionary meanings.

**Answer:** bushfires

**Activity 4:** Listen to the text again and say the following statements *True or False*.

a. A dry season is characterized by low rainfall and low humidity.

**Answer:** True
**Why:** A dry season is a yearly period of low rainfall (quoted in Lesson 6), and during it humidity is very low.

b. The tropical rain belt always lies in the southern hemisphere.

**Answer:** False
**Why:** The rain belt moves: it lies in the northern hemisphere for part of the year (see Activity 3b) and in the southern hemisphere for the other part.

c. Water holes and rivers dry up in the dry season.

**Answer:** True
**Why:** Because humidity and rainfall are very low in the dry season, some water holes and rivers dry up.

d. During the dry season, days are typically cloudy.

**Answer:** False
**Why:** During the dry season days are typically sunny/clear with few clouds, because there is little rain.

**Activity 2:** With a friend, look at the pictures and read the passage quickly to answer these questions.

1. What do the pictures show?

**Answer:** They show different methods of irrigation used in dry season farming.
**Why:** Picture 1: water flowing from pipes into furrows between rows of crops; Picture 2: drip lines (thin black tubes) along rows of young plants; Picture 3: plastic bottles hung on sticks dripping water onto plants.

2. What are the differences among the three pictures?

**Answer:** Picture 1 shows furrow (channel) irrigation, where water flows through pipes into long ditches between crop rows; Picture 2 shows drip irrigation with plastic tubes laid along the rows; Picture 3 shows bottle drip irrigation, where water drips slowly from plastic bottles hung above each plant.
**Why:** Description of the three pictures.

3. Which of these methods are practiced in your area?

**Answer:** (model answer) In my area farmers use furrow (channel) irrigation from the river; some also use drip irrigation for vegetables.
**Why:** Personal/local answer; any honest answer naming one of the three methods (or none) is acceptable.

4. What is the main idea of the passage?

**Answer:** Dry season farming (farming with irrigation in the dry season) has a long history in Ethiopia and is important because it improves food supply, food security and the country's economy.
**Why:** Paragraph 1 defines dry season farming, paragraph 2 says "Ethiopia has a long history of dry season farming", and paragraph 3 explains its benefits (food availability, less import dependence, exports, wheat production).

**Dry Season Farming in Ethiopia**

[Figure: three photos. (1) Rows of young maize plants with water running from two curved pipes out of a feeder channel into the furrows between the rows. (2) Young leafy green plants in reddish soil with thin black drip-irrigation tubes running along the rows. (3) A field of young plants, each with an upside-down plastic water bottle tied to a stick above it, dripping water down. Source: https://farmersreviewafrica.com/us-350000-granted-for-water-saving-drip-irrigation-in-nigeria/11/09/2021]

Dry season farming is often described as crop production with **irrigation** or water dripping during a dry season. **It** is practiced usually in regions that receive at least 20 inches (50cm) of annual rainfall, and **utilizes** the moisture stored in the soil from the rainy season.

Ethiopia has a long history of dry season farming. It has been used for years to **cultivate** crops like cotton, maize, sesame, sugarcane, vegetables and fruits around the Afar Region, along the Awash Basin.

(p.38)

Dry season farming improves food availability and ensures better pricing all year long. **It** also helps to reduce the **country's dependence** on imports as a way of ensuring food security. Dry season farmers can contribute significantly to the country's economic development through exporting food. For example, wheat is a crop that is cultivated in Ethiopia, and yet most of the wheat **consumed** in the country is imported mainly from America and Canada. However, the production of wheat can be increased by several millions of tones through dry season farming. In recent times, the Ethiopian government has increased **its** participation in dry season farming. **It** especially has increased its **investment** in the production of *yekola sinde* (meaning 'wheat from dry areas'), especially in the Afar Region.

[review: "tones" in the passage is a misspelling of "tonnes"]
