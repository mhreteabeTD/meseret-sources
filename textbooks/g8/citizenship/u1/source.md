### 1.1.1 Ethics and virtue ethics

**At the end of this section, you will be able to:**

**Brainstorming:** What do you think of virtue or virtue ethics?

**Answer:** (model answer) Virtue ethics is about developing good character so that one becomes a good citizen in an organised community; virtues are good habits or dispositions (like honesty, courage, generosity) that help the community function well and serve the common good.
**Why:** The text that follows: "Virtue ethics is concerned with the development of one's character in order to prepare one for good citizenship in an organised community… It is the pursuit of understanding and living a moral life." and "Virtues are citizen dispositions, habits, or characteristics that are conducive or beneficial to the healthy functioning and common good of a community."

Virtue ethics is concerned with the development of one's character in order to prepare one for good citizenship in an organised community, with the belief that a community composed of people of good character would be a good community. It is the pursuit of understanding and living a moral life.

Virtues are citizen dispositions, habits, or characteristics that are conducive or beneficial to the healthy functioning and common good of a community. In other words, these dispositions encompass all positive behaviours and actions.

(p.6)

In society, responsible citizens are expected to exhibit qualities such as courage, generosity, and honesty, among others.

This character based approach to morality holds that virtue is acquired through practice. Moral virtue does not come naturally to us. Moral virtues are habits that we develop through practice and imitation of exceptionally virtuous people or especially virtuous actions. It believes that virtue ethics provides an account of what is right and wrong based on what a "virtuous agent" would do. A virtuous person is one whose character traits are all virtues or good and has no vices or bad.

- ⓘ "A virtue," or "virtues," is specific character traits that tend to make a person virtuous, or good. For example, honesty, compassion, courage, and so on.
- ⓘ The term "vice" is often associated with bad habits. Vices are the polar opposite of virtues, and they refer to evil or personal corruption. For instance, dishonesty, cowardice, or selfishness, injustice, and so on.

**Case study — The Fox in The Well**

A fox fell into a well once upon a time. He was not injured as a result of his fall. He was not in danger of drowning because there was little water in the well, but he couldn't get out. "Help! Help! Help! Help!" he cried, but no one could hear him. A wolf eventually passed by the well. He was awakened by the call. "Who is down there?" he inquired as he gazed into the well. "It's me," exclaimed the fox. "I am grateful that you have come to assist me." "How did you get down there?" the wolf inquired. "Have you been there a long time? Is the water quite deep? I pity you, poor fellow! That's not the place for you. I see you have a bad cold. I wish you were there." "Please don't talk to me," the fox cried. "I require assistance. Get me out, and I'll know you're sorry for what happened to me." [review: "I wish you were there" is as printed; it does not make clear sense (probably meant "I wish you were out of there").]

**?** What do we mean by the term 'ethics'? Before the lesson, take a few moments to write down a definition of what you think the term means.

**Answer:** Ethics is the study of what good and bad ends to pursue in life and what is right and wrong to do in daily life.
**Why:** p.8: "The study of what good and bad ends to pursue in life, as well as what is right and wrong to do in daily life, is known as ethics."

(p.8)

The study of what good and bad ends to pursue in life, as well as what is right and wrong to do in daily life, is known as ethics. It is the creation of reasonable standards and procedures for making ethical decisions. On the other hand, virtue ethics is a branch of ethics developed primarily by Aristotle and other ancient Greek thinkers that emphasises the role of an individual's character and virtues in determining the rightness of an action, as opposed to approaches that emphasise duties or rules or the consequences of actions.

Every day, we make decisions. Every morning, moral issues greet us; they confront us individually, at school or in our daily lives. Dealing with moral issues is sometimes challenging. What questions should we ask and how should we think about an ethical issue? What factors should we take into account? This will bring us to steps in ethical decision making.

**?** Are you the type of person who always tries to do the right thing? How do you know what is the "right thing" to do?

**Answer:** (model answer) Yes, I try to do the right thing. I know what is right by thinking about my values and what a good (virtuous) person would do, and by following the steps of ethical decision making: identify the problem, think of who will be affected, look at the possible solutions, weigh them, and choose and justify the best one.
**Why:** p.6: virtue ethics gives "an account of what is right and wrong based on what a 'virtuous agent' would do"; p.9 gives the five "Steps in Ethical Decision Making".

(p.9)

**Steps in Ethical Decision Making**

How do people try to solve ethical issues by making the right decisions or choices? The answer lies in ethical decision making methods. There are five simple and inseparable steps to solving an ethical problem.

1. **Identify the central ethical problem:** The first step is to undertake research, investigation, and study until the whole problem is understood.
2. **Identify stakeholders and their interests:** Determine who will be affected by the decisions made. This can be anyone who is affected by the problem and is involved in the outcome of the problem.
3. **Find a possible solution to the dilemma:** This step is to develop and solve the entire set of alternatives. The information gathered will be used to develop an alternative course of action.
4. **Evaluate each solution:** Weigh the strengths and weaknesses of each option and select the alternative with the most strengths and weaknesses. [review: as printed "most strengths and weaknesses"; the meaning should be "the most strengths and the fewest weaknesses".]
5. **Select and justify the solution that best solves the dilemma:** This step makes a selection. You need to choose the best option.

(p.10)

**Activity 3**

Personal research and Group work.
- Identify certain ethical problems in your area or locality and discuss in group on how to apply the five steps in ethical decision making.

**Answer:** (model answer) Example problem: students cheating in exams at our school.
1. Identify the central ethical problem: cheating is dishonest and unfair to students who study; find out how and why it happens.
2. Identify stakeholders and their interests: the students who cheat, honest students, teachers, the school and parents — all want fair results and real learning.
3. Find possible solutions: report cheating, separate seats in exams, give study help/tutoring, run awareness talks on honesty, set penalties.
4. Evaluate each solution: weigh the strengths and weaknesses of each (e.g. penalties stop cheating but do not help weak students; tutoring helps learning but takes time).
5. Select and justify the best solution: combine tutoring/study groups with clear exam rules, because it removes the reason to cheat and keeps exams fair for everyone.
(Other local problems can be used: corruption, littering, theft, abuse of public property.)
**Why:** Applies the five steps on p.9 in order.

**Virtue ethics**

Rather than focusing on following rules, the emphasis in virtue ethics is on developing oneself as a good person. For example, suppose you were given the opportunity to donate to a charity. Donating, according to virtue ethicists, is the type of action that a virtuous person would take. Another example is deciding whether to lie or tell the truth; virtue ethicists ask whether they want to be honest or dishonest. Virtue ethicists' value being a person who is honest, trustworthy, and generous, as well as other virtues that leads to a good life, over one's ethical duty or obligations.

**Activity 4**

Think pair activity.
- Do you think of yourself as a "good person"? How do you intend to demonstrate in your area or community? Discuss your concept with your partner.

**Answer:** (model answer) Yes, I try to be a good person, and I can become better through practice. I will show it by being honest (telling the truth, not cheating), generous (sharing with and helping people in need), compassionate (listening to and encouraging others), fair (treating everyone equally), and by taking part in community work such as cleaning the area or helping elders.
**Why:** p.10: "the emphasis in virtue ethics is on developing oneself as a good person"; virtue ethicists value "being a person who is honest, trustworthy, and generous"; p.6: virtue "is acquired through practice".

Compassion is an empathic understanding of another person's feelings, accompanied by unselfishness or a desire to act on that person's behalf. Compassion is the ability to empathise with others and care about their well-being, and it is thus an essential characteristic of citizens in a society dedicated to the common good. For example, our friends may require words of encouragement or appreciation, someone may require the gift of being listened to, or someone may simply require to be understood. People who are compassionate in spirit are willing to appreciate those with whom they may disagree on some issues; we can show compassion in a variety of ways. There would be far less hunger, conflict, homelessness, and unhappiness if we showed more compassion to one another and to our community.

Two sisters lived in the woods. The younger one went to draw water from the spring one day and was met by a poor old woman who asked for a drink of water. The younger sister kindly gave the old woman a drink. The older sister also went to draw water from the spring and was met by a beautiful but thirsty princess who asked for a drink of water. The older sister refused and told the princess to fetch her own water.

(p.19)

Generosity: at home, a student shares food and gives old but good clothes and books to a needy neighbour's child; in the community, students give their time to clean the area or donate blood (Figure 1.14). **Why:** Compassion is "the ability to empathise with others and care about their well-being"; generosity is "a moral disposition to expend time, effort, and resources for the benefit of others"; self-discipline is following acceptable standards "freely and on our own initiative … in the absence of any external controls."

## Glossary

- **Compassion:** is the ability to understand or feel what another person is going through from within their own frame of reference, or the ability to put oneself in another's shoes.
- **Courage:** is the attitude of confronting and dealing with what is perceived to be difficult or painful rather than withdrawing from it.
- **Ethics:** is a field of study which is concerned with what is good or bad, right or wrong actions.
- **Fairness:** is frequently defined as a method of evaluating people or situations that is free of bias.
- **Generosity:** is the moral disposition to expend time, effort, and resources for the benefit of others.
- **Honesty:** is defined as the practice of not telling or accepting lies, as well as being truthful or accurate in one's actions.
- **Prudence:** is a type of practical reasoning, the ability to objectively examine the potential consequences of your actions and control yourself based on that examination.
- **Self-Discipline:** is the ability to behave and work in a controlled manner, which includes adhering to specific rules or standards.
- **Virtue ethics:** offers an account of right and wrong based on what a 'virtuous agent' would do.

(p.40)

## Unit Review Questions

Do these review exercises in your exercise book

### Part I: True or False Items

1. Human beings have the same level of virtues and characters.

**Answer:** False
**Why:** Virtues are not equal in everyone because they are not inborn: "Moral virtue does not come naturally to us. Moral virtues are habits that we develop through practice and imitation" (1.1.1, p.6), so people develop different levels of virtue and character depending on their practice. [review: no single sentence in the unit states this directly; answer inferred from the p.6 statement that virtue is acquired through practice.]

2. Virtue ethics qualities are important in creating a positive attitude.

**Answer:** True
**Why:** "It also helps in building a positive approach to things: An effective Virtue ethics would help learners develop a positive attitude towards difficult situations and boost their self-esteem." (p.34); the Unit summary also lists "developing a positive attitude" as a benefit.

3. The best policy is honesty, which is the habit of telling the truth.

**Answer:** True
**Why:** Honesty is "the behaviour and attitude of not telling or accepting lies" and "Being truthful in what you say and do is what honesty entails" (1.2.1); "Being regarded as an honest person by society is one of the best compliments a person can receive."

4. Fairness means everyone receives and shares exactly the same thing.

**Answer:** False
**Why:** "We must recognize that being fair does not always imply treating everyone the same way in every situation." (p.22). Fairness means treating people equitably "based on their merits and abilities" and free of bias.

5. To stand up and give a speech in front of the class requires courage.

**Answer:** True
**Why:** Courage "is the attitude of confronting and dealing with what is perceived to be difficult or painful rather than avoiding it; it is a quality of being fearless or brave" (1.2.2). Speaking in front of the class is something many find difficult or frightening, so doing it requires courage.

### Part II: Multiple Choices Questions

1. Which one of the following is not a way of participating in public government?
   A. Becoming a member of associations or clubs.
   B. Debate on issues of importance and concern.
   C. Showing apathy to issues of prime concern
   D. Reading and writing about issues

**Answer:** C. Showing apathy to issues of prime concern
**Why:** The text lists "Becoming a member of association or clubs", "Debating on issues of major concern" and "Reading and writing about issues" as ways to participate (p.31). Apathy means lack of interest, which is the opposite of the "active participation" democracy requires.

(p.41)

2. ______ evaluates action in terms of virtues or good character one possesses.
   A. Vices
   B. Virtue ethics
   C. Ethics
   D. All are correct answers

**Answer:** B. Virtue ethics
**Why:** "Virtue ethics is concerned with the development of one's character" (p.5) and "virtue ethics provides an account of what is right and wrong based on what a 'virtuous agent' would do" (p.6; Glossary). Vices are bad habits, and Ethics in general is the study of good/bad, right/wrong actions, not specifically in terms of virtues.

3. Among the following, which one is the consequence of dishonesty?
   A. Results in respect among friends and the community.
   B. Erodes trust among friends and the community.
   C. An honest person earns a good status among the community.
   D. It is one of the best compliments a person can receive.

**Answer:** B. Erodes trust among friends and the community.
**Why:** A, C and D are results of honesty ("Everyone respects someone who is truthful. Being regarded as an honest person by society is one of the best compliments a person can receive"). Dishonesty does the opposite: "a lack of honesty is a way for disaster" and it destroys trust.

4. Which one of the following is not an example of showing compassion?
   A. Helping someone in pain
   B. Leaving a form of gift
   C. Understanding another personal feeling
   D. Expressing appreciation to another person

**Answer:** B. Leaving a form of gift
**Why:** Compassion is "an empathic understanding of another person's feelings, accompanied by unselfishness or a desire to act on that person's behalf" (1.2.3): helping someone in pain (A), understanding another person's feelings (C) and giving "words of encouragement or appreciation" (D) are named as ways of showing compassion. Leaving a gift is giving things, which the book links to generosity, not to empathising with someone's feelings. [review: option B is ambiguous — the compassion text mentions "the gift of being listened to", and giving can also express compassion; B is the intended answer only by elimination.]

5. Which one of the following is different from the others?
   A. Paying fair tax
   B. Being informed
   C. Self-discipline
   D. Participation in government

**Answer:** C. Self-discipline
**Why:** Paying fair taxes, staying informed and participating in public government are the three examples of civic virtues in 1.2.8 (pp.29–31). Self-discipline is a personal moral virtue (1.2.6), not one of the listed civic virtues.

(p.42)

### Part IV: Give Short answers for the following questions.

1. Write the importance of generosity for oneself, friends, and the community?

**Answer:** (model answer) For oneself: being generous makes us feel good because we know we have made someone happy; it is a natural confidence booster and a natural repellent of self-hatred. For friends: it fosters social connections and strengthens relationships — when we give to others we bring them closer to us and ourselves closer to them. For the community: being generous and kind encourages us to see others in a more positive light and fosters a sense of community and interconnectedness; a virtuous citizen is generous to others and the community as a whole.
**Why:** "Being generous can make people feel good because they know they have made someone happy. Generosity is a natural confidence booster as well as a natural repellent of self-hatred. It also fosters social connections and strengthens relationships. When we give to others, we not only bring them closer to us, but we also bring ourselves closer to them … being generous and kind encourages us to see others in a more positive light and fosters a sense of community, a sense of interconnectedness." (1.2.4, p.20)

2. Explain the importance of qualities and principles of virtue ethics?

**Answer:** (model answer) Qualities and principles of virtue ethics (honesty, courage, compassion, generosity, fairness, self-discipline, prudence, civic virtue): (1) help one to distinguish between right and wrong and teach values such as diversity, tolerance, mutual respect and pluralism; (2) enable one to become a responsible and contributing member of society and strengthen the social cohesion of a community; (3) help in finding out the true purpose of life, since we as a society rely on one another to survive; (4) help in building a positive approach to things, a positive attitude towards difficult situations and self-esteem; (5) help to eliminate social evils such as gender discrimination, oppression, violence and racial discrimination. They are decisive for the well-being of the individual and the society.
**Why:** These are the reasons given in 1.3 (pp.33–34) and in the Unit summary: "If students are fully acquainted with virtue ethics qualities and principles such as honesty, courage, fairness, compassion, and generosity, they will be decisive for the well being of the individual and the society."

3. Describe the ways in which you can demonstrate courage in your daily life.

**Answer:** (model answer) By trying again and again until I succeed instead of giving up after failing (e.g. studying harder after a poor exam result); by working hard despite difficulties or obstacles; by facing difficult or frightening tasks instead of avoiding them, such as speaking in front of the class or answering questions; by standing up for what is right, such as telling the truth when it is hard, refusing to cheat, or defending a classmate who is being bullied; and by admitting my mistakes.
**Why:** Courage "is the attitude of confronting and dealing with what is perceived to be difficult or painful rather than avoiding it … It means trying again and again until you succeed. It is the determination to work hard despite any odds or obstacles that may exist." and "A courageous person never loses heart despite failing once or twice." (1.2.2, pp.15–16)

4. Discuss the ways in which you can demonstrate qualities of virtue Ethics in your daily life.

**Answer:** (model answer) Honesty: telling the truth and not cheating or stealing. Courage: not giving up when things are difficult. Compassion: listening to and helping people in difficulty. Generosity: sharing time, effort and things with others, e.g. donating blood or helping a needy classmate. Fairness: treating everyone equally and without favouritism. Self-discipline: following rules on my own, doing homework on time, keeping hygiene. Prudence: thinking before acting and learning from mistakes. Civic virtue: staying informed, participating in clubs and community meetings, protecting the environment (e.g. planting trees) and, as an adult, paying fair tax and voting.
**Why:** 1.4 asks students to connect "previous learning in class with the outside world (i.e. extracurricular activities, at your home and in the community)"; Figure 1.14 shows "Students … practising virtue through donating blood, participating in environmental protection and keeping their hygiene in the school compound"; each example follows the definitions of the virtues given in 1.2.1–1.2.8.
