With regard to the origin of human beings, there are two theories suggested.

These are: creationist theory and scientific (evolution) theory

Creationist theory said that human beings were created by God. Those who believe in the assumption that God creates humans and the whole universe are Creationists.

The Scientific theory believes that human beings and other living things are the result of gradual and natural process of changes that took millions of years. Charles Darwin (1809-1882) explained about evolutionary process.

Hominids were the ancestors of human beings. Human beings belong to the species called homo sapiens. Australopithecines (southern ape) were the earliest (the first) (p.34) human species that appeared in Africa around 6 million years ago. ### 2.1.3. Historical paths human kind passes over time

There are different species that belong to genus homo: homohabilis, homo erectus and homo sapiens.

The homo habilis (handy human being) is considered to be the oldest human being that appeared in Africa. Its fossils were found at Olduvia Gorge in Tanzana, at lake Turkan in Kenya and in the Omo valley in Ethiopia.

[review: book spelling errors — "Olduvia Gorge in Tanzana" should be "Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania", "lake Turkan" should be "Lake Turkana"; "homohabilis" = Homo habilis.]

Home erectus (up right walking human being) was the first human being to master the use of fire and the earliest species to migrate from Africa to other part of the world. Its fossils were discovered in Ethiopia in the Omo Basin, Melka-Kunture, Konso-Gardula and Middle Awash.

[review: book typo — "Home erectus" should read "Homo erectus".]

Homo sapiens (wise human being) were very similar to modern human being. Fossils of this species were found in the Awash and Omo valleys and in the areas near Dire Dawa. Modern beings appeared about 40,000 years ago in Africa. They are different in many ways from their ancestors. They have large brains, are bi-pedal and wake erect, use languages, develop culture, invent tools and transmit knowledge from generation to generation

[review: "wake erect" is a book typo for "walk erect". Also, "Modern beings appeared about 40,000 years ago in Africa" is out of date — the oldest Homo sapiens fossils (e.g. Omo Kibish, Ethiopia ~195,000–230,000 years; Jebel Irhoud ~300,000 years) are far older. Kept as printed.]

**Answer:** Human population is the total number of human inhabitants of a specified area, such as a city, country or continent, at a given time.

- What factors have contributed to the growth of world population?

**Answer:** Advancements in scientific knowledge, agriculture, industry and medicine; improvement in people's living conditions; humans' ability to control famine and disease; and the relatively lower cost of importing vaccines, antibiotics, insecticides and high-yielding varieties of seeds.

Human population refers to the total number of human inhabitants of a specified area, such as a city, country, or continent, at a given time. Population is one of the most critical (p.38) factor that determine the socio-economic and environmental conditions of every country in the world. The world's population has been experiencing continuous growth since the 15th century. The UNDP estimated that world population reached 6 billion with an annual growth rate of 1.5% by 2000 A.D. The number added to world population per year would increase from 80 million to 90 million if the growth rate of 1.5% continues, and world population would double in a period of 40-50 years from about 2000 A.D. Such rapid and dramatic growth rate of the world population is known as **population explosion**.

##### Start-up questions

Fertility refers to the actual reproductive performance of a population. The growth of world population depends largely on human fertility. This is so because any society replenishes itself through the process of human fertility.

**Measures of fertility**

Fertility can be determined using different methods, but the most commonly used measure of fertility is birth rate.

**Birth Rate (BR)**

Birth rate is the most common and simplest index of fertility. It is simply the number of live births observed in one year among one thousand people in a given region.

> Birth rate can be expressed as a number of live births per thousand people.
>
> **BR = (B / P) × K**
>
> Where **B** is the total number of live births during a year, **P** is mid-year total population and **K** is a constant, conventionally 1000.
>
> **Example**
>
> If the number of live births in a population of 50,686,000 was 150,800, the birth rate is:
>
> BR = (B / P) × K = (150,800 / 50,686,000) × 1000 = 2.9 births per1000 per year

[added step: 150,800 ÷ 50,686,000 = 0.002975…
0.002975… × 1000 = 2.975…
≈ 3.0 births per 1000 per year (to 1 decimal place).]

[review: book says 2.9; correct rounding of 2.975 is 3.0 (book truncated). Also the numbers are unrealistic: a real birth rate for a country of ~50 million (e.g. Ethiopia) is around 30–40 per 1000, so the births figure was probably meant to be about 1,508,000. Keep the method; flag the result.]

**Factors of Fertility Distribution**

Various factors affect fertility distribution in the world including

- Low access to contraceptives;
- Backwardness and poverty;
- Low status of women;

(p.43)

- Need of children for labour;
- Children are considered as symbol of virility and
- Low educational background.

#### B. Mortality

##### Start-up questions

- What is mortality?

**Answer:** Mortality refers to the occurrence of death in the human population.
**Why:** Book definition, p.43.

- What factors affect mortality?

Mortality refers to the occurrence of death in the human population. Mortality rates are higher in the developing countries than in the developed ones. This difference is caused by variations in standards of living, nutrition, medical services, personal hygiene and environmental sanitation.

**Measures of Mortality**

Mortality can be measured in a number of ways, including the use of death rate and infant mortality rate.

**Death rate (DR):** is the ratio of the total registered deaths of a specified year in a region to the total mid-year population, multiplied by 1000.

(p.44)

> **DR = (D / P) × K** where DR is crude death rate, D stands for total observed deaths, P is total mid-year population, and k is a constant, 1000.
>
> **Example**
>
> In a hypothetical region, the total number of deaths observed in 2011 was 21,000, and the total mid-year population was 7,000,000. What is death rate?
>
> DR = (D / P) × K = (21,000 / 7,000,000) × 1000 = 3/1000. This number indicates that the deaths of 3 persons were observed for each 1000 people per year.

[added step: 21,000 ÷ 7,000,000 = 0.003
0.003 × 1000 = 3 deaths per 1000 people per year.]

**Rate of natural increase = Birth rate − Death rate**

Infant mortality is the death of young children aged below one year.

Major factors that attributed for high death rate are the following:

- Low standard of living
- Low access to health facilities
- Poor nutrition, and sanitary practices
- Civil war and political instability
- Wide spread of famine and starvation
- High incidence of disease and infections

#### C. Migration

##### Start-up questions

- What is migration?

**Answer:** Migration refers to the movement of humans from one place to another.
**Why:** Book definition, p.45.

- Why do people migrate?

**Answer:** Because of push factors (pressures such as poor employment, civil war and harsh climate) and pull factors (attractions such as good job opportunity, favorable climate and good welfare service).
**Why:** p.45, push and pull factors I and II.

Migration refers to the movement of humans from one place to another. Migration takes place with two major factors. These are:

I. **Push factors**: impose pressure on peoples to migrate, which includes poor employment, civil war, and harsh climate etc.

II. **Pull factors:** attract peoples to migrate, which include good job opportunity, favorable climate and good welfare service.

An emigrant (out migrant) is a person who is leaving one country to live in another.

An immigrant (in migrant) is a person who is entering a country from another to make a new home.

**Measures of migration**

**Immigration rate** indicates the number of people arriving at a destination per 1000 people in a given year.

> **Immigration rate = (Number of Immigrants / Total population at destination) × 1000**

**Emigration rate** indicates the number of departing people from an area of origin per 1000 people in a given year.

> **Emigration rate = (Number of Emigrants / Total population at origin) × 1000**

(p.46)

**Net Migration Rate (NMR)**: shows the net effect (balance) of immigration and emigration in an area. It can be expressed as an increase or decrease per 1000 people in the area in a given year.

> **NMR = ((Number of Immigrants − Number of Emigrants) / Total population) × 1000**
>
> **Example:**
>
> Assume the number of emigrants and immigrants of country 'A' are 50,000 and 260,000 respectively. If the total population is 105 million, what will be the **NMR**?
>
> NMR = ((Number of Immigrants − Number of Emigrants) / Total population) × 1000 = ((260,000 − 50,000) / 105,000,000) × 1000 = 2/1000

[added step: 260,000 − 50,000 = 210,000
210,000 ÷ 105,000,000 = 0.002
0.002 × 1000 = 2 per 1000 (a net gain of 2 people per 1000 per year).]

- The nature of topography (slope and altitude)
- Climate condition (mainly rainfall and temperature)

### IV. Short answer

Direction: Give short answers for the following questions.

1. What are the main factors that control the change in population size?

**Answer:** Fertility, mortality and migration.
**Why:** p.41: these three components of population dynamics, combined, control "the changes in population size and composition".

2. Write down some factors affecting fertility distribution?

**Answer:** Low access to contraceptives; backwardness and poverty; low status of women; need of children for labour; children considered as a symbol of virility; low educational background.
**Why:** "Factors of Fertility Distribution", pp.42–43.

3. Describe the two important statistical tools to understand age structure.

**Answer:** (1) Age groups — dividing the population into young age 0–14 (young dependents), working age 15–64 (economically active) and old age 65+ (elderly dependents). (2) Population pyramid — a graph of the age distribution of a population by sex.
**Why:** p.47, "Age groups" and "Population Pyramid".

4. Explain the difference between urban and rural settlement.

**Answer:** Rural settlements are villages and scattered settlements far from urban centres where people mainly practise agriculture, with low density and few services; urban settlements are towns and cities where people do non-agricultural work, with higher population and a wider range of goods and services.
**Why:** p.54 definitions and settlement hierarchy (Figure 2.5).

5. Explain the socio economic implication of road traffic accidents.

**Answer:** Socially, they cause loss of lives, injuries and permanent disability, and increase dependency and poverty. Economically, they destroy property and cause losses through treatment costs, lost productivity, lost working time of victims and relatives, and loss of skilled labour force.
**Why:** p.65 paragraph 2.

(p.71)
