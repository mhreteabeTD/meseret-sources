## Unit 1: Introduction to Career and Technical Education (CTE)

### Learning Outcomes

At the end of this unit, learners will be able to:

- Define the concepts of career and job.
- Describe Career and Technical Education.
- Differentiate between career and job.
- Describe the importance of Career and Technical Education.

### Key Words

- **Career:** *Career can be briefly defined as the path of progress individuals follow to realize their aspiration.*
- **Job:** *A job is any legal activity that allows an individual to perform a service or produce goods and in return earn money she/he can use to buy things.*
- **Technical Education:** *Education which has a primary focus on practical or technical skills for performing a certain task.*
- **Career and Technical Education:** *The practice of teaching skills-based careers to students.*

(p.2)

## 1.1 Meaning of Career

*(Section 1.1 starts on book p.2.)*

### Brainstorming Question

**In what type of job do you want to engage in the future? Why?**

**Answer:** [open question] Model answer: "I want to become a doctor (or teacher, engineer, farmer, etc.) because I want to help people in my community and grow in that field over my life."
**Why:** The book says a career is "the path of progress individuals follow to realize their aspiration – life goals (what they want to be in the future)" (p.2, §1.1, para 1). Any job the learner names, with a reason linked to their own interests and goals, is acceptable.

*Career* can be briefly defined as the path of progress individuals follow to realize their aspiration - life goals (what they want to be in the future) during their education and work life.

For a person on a job, career comprises of the *different jobs a person has held, titles/positions he/she has earned and work he/she has accomplished over a long period of time.* Thus, a career includes *one's choice of field of work and advancement* in the chosen field. A career could mean working as a high school director, teacher, doctor, or hairstylist, etc.

## 1.2 Job vs Career

*(Section 1.2 starts on book p.2.)*

As presented above, career describes an individual's journey through learning, work, and other aspects of life. What about a job? Are the concepts "Job" and "Career" the same?

### Activity 1

List the types of jobs in your locality and show to your teacher.

**Answer:** [open question] Model answer: teacher, farmer, trader/shopkeeper, driver, carpenter, tailor, hairdresser, nurse, mechanic, construction worker (daily labourer), cleaner, plumber.
**Why:** The book defines a job as "any legal activity that allows an individual to perform a service or produce goods and in return earn money" (Key Words, p.1), and gives examples such as cleaner, plumber and teacher (p.4). Any legal paid work seen in the learner's area fits.

(p.3)

It is often said that "job" and "career" are the same. Though they are related, they, however, mean two different things. A career is often confused with a job. Though career and job are related, they are different (see Table 1.1) below.

*Table 1.1: Differences between Career and Job*

| Career | Job |
|---|---|
| Career is a series of connected employment opportunities. | Job is something that individuals do at a particular period of time. |
| Career is being engaged in a satisfying and productive activity throughout one's Lifetime. | Job is what a person does at work now to bring home an income (money). |
| Career involves a long-term view of a series of jobs and work experiences. | Job is a regular activity in exchange of payment. |
| Career is long-term professional journey determined based on your passions. | Job can be full-time or part-time and may be short-term. |
| The course of career changes through time as one can move from one job to another. | Relatively stable as one can work for a long time. |

(p.4)

Thus, a career has a goal of improving your skills so you can grow in your job, or move into another job. Career involves not only what one does now but also future advancement in the current field of job, including changing to other jobs.

We often hear people saying: "I have a job as a cleaner."

"I have a job as a plumber." "I have a job as a teacher."

By saying so, they are telling what they are doing at the moment, i.e., their current job. A job to be a career, one has to work on the same job over the years. A career can be one job or series of jobs obtained through advancement.

## 1.3 Meaning of Career and Technical Education

*(Section 1.3 starts on book p.6.)*

Career and Technical Education (CTE) is a broad term of education that combines academic and technical skills with the knowledge and training needed for further education and employment opportunity. Career and Technical Education is the practice of teaching specific technology-based and career-oriented skills to students. CTE offers skills to begin working in different economic sectors.

CTE combines academic education (education which has learning as its primary purpose) and technical education (education which has a primary focus on practical or technical skills for performing a certain task). For employment opportunity, CTE prepares students for the world of work by introducing them to workplace competencies (knowledge, skills, and attitudes) in a real-world applied context.

### Activity 3

Is education from Grade 1 - 6 academic or technical? Why?

**Answer:** [open question] Model answer: It is mainly academic, because in Grades 1–6 the main purpose is learning general knowledge (reading, writing, mathematics, science, languages), not practical skills for performing a certain job task. Some subjects (e.g. crafts or agriculture practice) include a little practical work.
**Why:** The book says academic education is "education which has learning as its primary purpose" and technical education "has a primary focus on practical or technical skills for performing a certain task" (p.6, para 2).

(p.7)

## 1.4 Importance of Career and Technical Education

*(Section 1.4 starts on book p.7.)*

Career and Technical Education covers courses and programs in agriculture, construction, pottery, leather processing, metal work, pharmacy, crafts, hotel service, business, etc. It plays a vital role in human resource development of a country by creating skilled manpower, enhancing socioeconomic development and improving the quality of life. CTE programs are the primary college and career-readiness programs, providing both technical training and hands-on learning experiences for students to practice and develop cross-cutting skills that all employers value. CTE equips students with the types of technical trainings and practical work experiences that are applicable across a variety of fields.

### Unit Review Questions

**I. Say "True" for the correct statements and "False" for the incorrect ones.**

1. Career is a one-time activity.

**Answer:** False.
**Why:** "Getting a job is a one-time event; a career is a lifelong process" (p.4, last para).

2. Career and job are not related.

**Answer:** False.
**Why:** "Though career and job are related, they are different" (p.3, para 1). A career is made up of one job or a series of jobs (p.4).

3. CTE does not involve acquiring knowledge.

**Answer:** False.
**Why:** CTE "combines academic and technical skills with the knowledge and training needed for further education and employment" and introduces workplace competencies "(knowledge, skills, and attitudes)" (p.6).

4. Career is for immediate gain but job is future oriented.

**Answer:** False.
**Why:** It is the other way round: a job is what a person does "now to bring home an income", while a career "involves a long-term view" and "future advancement" (Table 1.1, p.3; p.4, para 1).

5. CTE focuses more on academic learning.

**Answer:** False.
**Why:** CTE combines academic and technical education and is "the practice of teaching specific technology-based and career-oriented skills"; it gives "technical training and hands-on learning experiences" (pp.6–7).

[review: Q5 — the book does not say directly which CTE focuses on "more"; "False" follows from its stress on technical, hands-on and career-oriented skills.]

2. What one wants to be in the future is ____.

**Answer:** career (one's aspiration / life goal).
**Why:** "Career can be briefly defined as the path of progress individuals follow to realize their aspiration - life goals (what they want to be in the future)" (p.2).

3. ____ links school learning with the world of work.

**Answer:** Career and Technical Education (CTE).
**Why:** "CTE connects classroom learning with applied knowledge and skills to solve real problems" and "prepares students for the world of work" (pp.6–7).

**III. Answer the following question in writing.**

1. Describe the relationship and difference of job and career.

**Answer:** Job and career are related because a career is made up of one job or a series of jobs obtained through advancement. They differ because a job is what a person does now, at a particular period, to earn an income; it can be full-time or part-time and short-term, and getting it is a one-time event. A career is a long-term, lifelong process of progress (like climbing a ladder) in a chosen field, including the different jobs, positions and experiences a person gains over time and future advancement.
**Why:** Drawn from p.3 para 1 ("related … different"), Table 1.1 (p.3), and p.4 ("A career can be one job or series of jobs obtained through advancement"; "Getting a job is a one-time event; a career is a lifelong process").
