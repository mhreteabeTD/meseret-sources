# UNIT 1 — INFORMATION SYSTEMS AND THEIR APPLICATIONS

### Learning Outcomes

At the end of this unit, students will be able to:

- Explain ethical and legal issues in information systems
- Explain environmental, health, and social issues in information system
- Recognize the basics of intellectual property rights
- Describe digital identity management
- Appreciate the role of digital technologies in citizenship

### Unit Overview

Information systems have had an impact far beyond the world of business. New technologies create new situations that we have never dealt with before. The introduction of new technology can have a profound effect on human behavior. New technologies also give us capabilities that we did not have before, which in turn, create environments and situations that have not been specifically addressed in ethical terms. Users should also be aware of the consequences and implications of information systems' use and access on the one hand and maintaining the rights of creators and innovators on the other. This unit deals with the fundamentals of ethical, legal, social, environmental and health issues, intellectual property rights, digital identity management, and the role of digital technologies in citizens' engagement.

## 1.1. Ethical, Legal, Social, Environmental and Health Issues in the Use of Information Systems

The use of new technologies including information systems, has a significant impact on human behavior and our daily activities including how we live, work, and learn. Increased consequences have been emerging which needs all user's awareness about the use of information systems. The knowledge and skills about raising issues and proper use of information systems help students to become responsible members of society who can navigate a knowledge based and technology-led economy. This sub unit will focus on the major issues in use of information systems.

### 1.1.1 Ethical and Legal Issues in Information System

### Brainstorming 1.1

- How could someone gain access to online banking account?
- What would be the consequences of such access?

**Answer:** Someone can gain access by stealing or guessing the password/PIN (weak passwords, shoulder-surfing), by phishing (fake emails, SMS or websites that trick the owner into typing credentials), by malware such as keyloggers, by SIM-swap or stolen phone, or by using an unlocked shared computer. Consequences: money stolen or transferred, identity theft and fraud in the owner's name, loss of private data, and legal action against the attacker because it is a crime.
**Why:**
- The text says private information "should be accessed using personal passwords", but accounts "can be accessed by unauthorized sources" (Privacy).
- Stolen personal details can be used "for malicious purposes" — identity theft (Information Security and Liability).
- Unauthorized access is both unethical and illegal, so it has legal consequences.

Ethics is all about the principles of making right or wrong decisions. According to Britannica dictionary, ethics defined as, the discipline concerned with what is morally good and bad and morally right and wrong. Ethical principles needs to be respected and if not might result in legal consequences for those cases who are against the applied laws. The role of ethics in information systems has become more crucial as a result of technology. Questions related to ethics and legality are essential in many industries. Many professionals including medical and education professionals, government officials, and business people have legal and ethical oversight to control how their professions function. Information systems, by contrast, lack standard that align with ethical concerns in the current frequently changing and dynamic technological ecosystem. The ethical and legal issues include privacy, security, information gathering and digital ownership related concerns. You will learn about some of the main ethical and legal issues you need to know about as you participate in a constantly changing the digital world.

The following are some ethical and legal issues confronting the current digital world.

(p.3)

**Privacy:** In today's digitized environment, information privacy relates to personal data stored on the computer system, has become a prime concern for everybody. Most people have their data spread throughout the digital systems, and private information should be accessed using personal passwords. However, personal accounts such as email, social media, and private bank accounts, can be accessed by unauthorized sources which create both ethical and legal concerns. It is morally wrong and unethical to view an individual's private information in anyways which may also end up being a legal issue. All practices and attempts in organizations to check their employee's computer habits also need to be implemented without incurring any damage to individual privacy.

[figure: Figure 1.1 Privacy Issues — a laptop, phone, monitor and desktop PC in the centre, surrounded by eight "eye" icons with red arrows pointing at the devices (people watching/spying on personal devices).]

**Digital Ownership:** It is ability to access and/or use and protect data, information, and knowledge about ourselves or things we own. The expansion of digital mediums has allowed information to flow more freely than before which makes content easily copied and pasted online. This makes digital ownership hard to control in the digital world. It is unethical to use somebody else's digital works without getting permission from the owner first since it ignores the right of the creator. Creators has right to allow or deny the use of the contents. This permission can promote sharing of knowledge and information, credits the source and protect the consumer from false information. Users resources use should consider such concerns to promote proper use and dissemination of information, and to protect themselves from legal consequences that arises from violating intellectual property ownership rights.

[figure: Figure 1.2: Digital Ownership — two hands holding up a screen showing binary digits 101001 / 011010 / 101011, with a pen beside it (data as something owned and protected).]

> **Links:** More information about intellectual property and related concepts in detail in Section 1.2. of this book.

(p.4)

**Information Gathering:** Many information system applications collect users' data and credentials as part of their operations. Such information gathering trends can lead to many ethical and legal issues depending on the purpose and level of information gathering approaches. The measure has opened a debate about what and why information can be gathered. This issue raises a question of consent which protect individuals against attempts to steal information.

[figure: Figure 1.3: Information Gathering — icons for audio files, charts, documents, images, video, Excel, pie chart, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and YouTube, all with arrows flowing into one desktop computer; padlocks are drawn on the arrows (data from many apps collected into one system; locks = need for protection/consent).]

Users need to understand and examine the intention of any information-gathering request by any service providers or third-party applications while they use the services. Such requests need special attention if those organizations, systems, or applications request personal information and credentials. This protects against any illegal and fraudulent information stealing attempts.

**Information Security and Liability:** Growing amounts of personal and financial information are being transferred and stored online as organizations and businesses depend more and more on electronic data and computer networks to carry out their daily operations. If and when a breach in information security happens, this might expose organizations and businesses to potential security liability. Such attempts might lead to identity theft, which happens when the personal details of innocent people to be harvested by a third party so that they can be used for malicious purposes. The increasing use of online tools and platforms such as blogs and instant messaging is another source of potential liability. Accepted procedures and rules are needed to provide conditions to increase the reliability of information systems. Users also need to report any security breach incidents that happened on their systems and tools to the concerned department/unit or expert in their organizations or the concerned government body promptly.

For example – in Ethiopia, government and private organizations should report any cyber security breaches or attacks to the designated government unit that is responsible for protecting our country's information and information infrastructure from harm.

(p.5)

The above-mentioned pressing ethical and legal issues are assumed to be evolving as technologies change over time, and it is recommended to assess and analyze issues over time, concerning ethical and legal aspects.

**Social Media and Gaming Addiction** - According to addiction center definition social media and gaming addiction is a behavioral addiction that is characterized as being overly concerned about social media and computer games, driven by an uncontrollable urge to log on to or use social media, and devoting so much time and effort to social media that it impairs other important life areas (See Figure 1.7). **Plagiarism** – Computers and information systems are known for their educational benefits, which are praised, but having access to all knowledge has drawbacks of its own. According to university of oxford, Plagiarism is presenting work or ideas from another source as your own, with or without consent of the original author, by incorporating it into your work without full acknowledgement.

[figure: Figure 1.8: Plagiarism — a masked thief with a fishing rod "hooking" a light bulb (an idea) from above a boy working at a computer.]

Plagiarism in the workplace and in academia can destroy an author's credibility and have significant consequences, such as dismissal from school or job loss. Students need to produce original works, use books and Internet resources as references and sources of insight, and give appropriate credit to authors when they refer to their works in any way.

## 1.2. Intellectual Property

1. What will be the possible legal consequences of violating ethical issues in our information systems use?

**Answer:** Breaking privacy, digital ownership or security rules can lead to: criminal charges and imprisonment or fines (e.g. under Ethiopia's Computer Crime Proclamation No. 958/2016 for illegal access, data theft or fraud); civil lawsuits and paying damages to victims (e.g. copyright or patent infringement); seizure of illegally copied material; organisations being held liable for data breaches (security liability, fines, loss of licence); and dismissal from school or job (e.g. for plagiarism or misuse of company data).

2. Discuss the emerging health issues associated with the current digital devices use and systems applications in our day-to-day life

3. What are the 3 common components of a digital collaborations? Discuses each component meaning and roles for effective digital collaboration engagements.

## Notes for lesson design

### Key ideas by subsection
- **Unit overview:** new technology creates situations that ethics and law have not yet dealt with. Users must balance their own access against creators' rights.
- **1.1.1 Ethical and legal issues:** ethics = right vs wrong, and unethical acts can become legal issues. The four issues are **Privacy** (unauthorized access to personal accounts; employee monitoring must not damage privacy), **Digital Ownership** (you need the creator's permission to use their digital works), **Information Gathering** (consent, and checking why an app wants your data or credentials) and **Information Security and Liability** (breaches lead to identity theft and organisational liability; report incidents promptly, in Ethiopia to INSA).
- **1.1.2 Social issues:** benefits are closing the digital divide, AI/AR, and platforms for business and entertainment. Negatives are socialization gaps, cyberbullying (incl. online gender-based violence), social media/gaming addiction, plagiarism (presenting others' work as your own without acknowledgement), cybercrime (INSA reports rising attacks; "cyber warrior" career) and jobs lost or created by automation (cashiers, data entry clerks, factory workers at risk; skills keep changing).
- **1.1.3 Environmental issues:** devices use power and resources and contain toxic cadmium, lead and mercury. E-waste management means collect → recover/recycle safely → dispose with minimal harm. Green/clean technology (solar, wind, EVs, smart meters) helps, and paperless systems and virtual collaboration cut deforestation and travel emissions.
- **1.1.4 Health issues:** health information systems give fast, quality data for planning, and mobile devices improve care delivery. Overuse causes sleep disorders, loss of attention, stress, musculoskeletal problems, eye strain, headaches and obesity.
- **1.2 Intellectual property:** these are intangible ownership rights (Proclamation 320/2003, EIPA, eipo.gov.et). There are four types. A **trade secret** is confidential business information (recipes, source code, customer lists). A **patent** is an exclusive, limited-time right to an invention in exchange for publishing it; not all software qualifies (it must be new, inventive and industrially applicable). **Copyright** covers original works of authorship (incl. software and GUIs) and is automatic, but you need registration to sue. A **trademark** is a sign that distinguishes goods or services; it can last indefinitely with use and registration is optional.
- **1.3 Digital identity management:** a digital identity is all the information about a person, organisation or device that exists online. Managing it means limiting what you share, checking and correcting stored data, and avoiding redundant data. Verification is done by database checks, documents or biometrics. Ethiopia's National ID (Fayda) is part of Digital Ethiopia 2025.
- **1.4 Digital collaboration:** this means working together through digital tools in real time across distance. There are three components: **people, collaborative tools, devices**. There are five tool categories: communication, documentation/file sharing, project management, data visualization, notetaking.
- **1.5 Digital citizenship:** the responsible use of technology to engage with society. It should be taught as do's rather than don'ts: improve your community, engage respectfully, make your voice heard, check sources, and build a positive digital footprint. Example: the 2021 #ItsMyDam campaign for GERD.

### Common student mistakes
- Confusing **patent** and **copyright** (inventions vs creative works), or thinking copyright needs registration to exist. It is automatic; registration is only needed to sue.
- Thinking trademarks expire like patents. They can last indefinitely while in use.
- Mixing up **trade secret** (kept secret) with **patent** (made public in exchange for protection).
- Treating plagiarism as only "copying word for word". Using someone else's *ideas* without acknowledgement is also plagiarism, even with the author's consent.
- Listing "Information Security" and "Privacy" as the same thing, or forgetting **Information Gathering / consent** as one of the four ethical-legal issues.
- Listing natural gas as a green technology (the book does this; it is a fossil fuel).
- Thinking digital identity is only usernames/passwords. It includes all online data, biometrics and device identities.
- For the components of digital collaboration, naming tool types (communication, file sharing…) instead of **people, tools, devices**.
- Seeing digital citizenship as only a list of prohibitions. The book stresses positive action.
- Thinking automation only destroys jobs. The book says it "often creates as many jobs as it destroys", but needs new skills.

### Ideas for interactive practice and pictures
- **Sorting (drag into bins):** sort scenarios into the four ethical-legal issues (Privacy / Digital Ownership / Information Gathering / Security & Liability). For example: "an app asks for your contacts and SMS to run a flashlight" → Information Gathering; "a hacker leaks a bank's customer data" → Security & Liability. Build a generator from a scenario bank.
- **Sorting:** classify negative social issues (socialization gap, cyberbullying, addiction, plagiarism, cybercrime, job loss) from short stories.
- **Matching:** IP type ↔ example (Coca-Cola recipe → trade secret; Google PageRank → patent; a novel / app source code / GUI → copyright; Ride logo → trademark), and IP type ↔ duration/registration rule. Randomise from a table of about 20 items.
- **True/false rapid-fire** on IP facts (e.g. "Copyright needs registration to exist" → False).
- **Matching:** collaboration tool ↔ category (Zoom, Slack → communication; Dropbox, SharePoint → file sharing; Trello, Asana, ClickUp → project management; Tableau, Power BI, Grafana → data visualization; Evernote, OneNote, Google Keep → notetaking).
- **Ordering steps:** e-waste management (collect → sort → recover/recycle safely → dispose of the remainder safely); patent application (prepare description and claims → file with EIPA online → formal exam → substantive exam for novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability → grant and publish in the IP Gazette); incident reporting (detect → contain/keep evidence → report to IT unit → report to INSA → follow up); Activity 1.8 collaboration workflow (create doc → share link → schedule meeting → co-edit with screen share → reflect).
- **Health "posture checker" picture:** an interactive drawing of a student at a desk or phone. Tap the body parts (neck, back, shoulders, arms, eyes, feet) to reveal the risk and the fix. Add a screen-time slider (hours per day) that shows the risk level and break reminders (e.g. 20-20-20 rule: every 20 min look 20 feet away for 20 s, so 6 hours of screen time means 18 breaks).
- **Digital-footprint builder:** students choose posts or actions and watch a "footprint" meter go positive or negative. This fits digital citizenship do's vs poor citizenship (bullying, careless posting, ignoring safety).
- **Privacy settings simulator:** a mock social profile where students decide which fields to make public, private or not share, with scoring based on "limit the data you give out".
- **Clickable diagrams:** Fig 1.14 (health information system hub with 10 stakeholders: tap each to see what data it sends or receives); Fig 1.13 (paper vs paperless: tap to compare deforestation and emissions); Fig 1.11 e-waste ring (tap a device type for its hazardous materials and how to dispose of it).
- **Jobs automation sort:** drag jobs into "at risk from automation", "created by technology" or "changed", then justify. Random draw from a list of about 30 jobs.
- **Phishing/information-gathering spot-the-red-flag:** show a fake SMS or app permission screen and let students click the suspicious parts.
